You are pouring money into ads, the clicks arrive steadily, but the number of people leaving their details fits on one hand. Before raising the budget or rewriting the ad, look again at where people land: the **landing page**. A good landing page doesn't need to be beautiful — it needs to answer the question already in the head of whoever just clicked your ad.

Here are nine principles proven across a great many campaigns.

## 1. One landing page, one goal

![One landing page, one goal](https://apia.toolswave.net/uploads/images/1789670269227-dbeef037.png)

A landing page is not a home page. It should have **exactly one desired action**: leave a phone number, book a consultation, or request a quote. Every extra option you add (browse more products, read the blog, follow the page) drags the conversion rate for the main goal down.

**Takeaway:** If your page has more than one kind of call-to-action button, remove some.

## 2. The headline must match the promise in the ad

![The headline must match the promise in the ad](https://apia.toolswave.net/uploads/images/1789670309862-64cd07c8.png)

Someone clicks an ad saying "30% off conversational English courses" and lands on a page about "a trusted language centre with 10 years of experience" — they leave immediately. This is called **message mismatch**, and it is the most common reason cost per lead goes through the roof.

**Takeaway:** Copy the exact keyword phrase from the ad into the landing page's H1.

## 3. The most important content belongs on the first screen

On a phone you have roughly one and a half screens before someone decides to scroll on or leave. That space must carry three things: **what you sell**, **a concrete benefit**, and **the action button**. Don't spend the entire first screen on a handsome banner that says nothing.

**Takeaway:** Open the page on a phone and don't scroll — if you can't tell what it is, neither can your customer.

## 4. The shorter the form, the better

Every input field is another reason to give up. For most industries, **name + phone number** is enough; ask the rest during the consultation call. If you genuinely must collect more, split it into two steps so the first looks effortless.

**Takeaway:** Delete every field your sales team doesn't actually use on the first call.

## 5. Give evidence, not adjectives

"Leading quality" convinces nobody. **Numbers, real photographs, reviews with names attached** do: "1,240 students have completed the course", a photo of an actual class, three testimonials with faces. Put social proof right next to the form, at the moment of hesitation.

**Takeaway:** Replace at least one hollow marketing sentence with a verifiable number.

## 6. Remove the risk of filling in the form

People are wary of leaving a phone number for fear of nuisance calls. Say plainly what happens next: *"We call within 24 hours, once, no spam."* A refund policy, a privacy commitment, or "free consultation, no obligation" all work the same way.

**Takeaway:** Write one line directly under the submit button describing exactly what happens after the click.

## 7. Load speed is part of your conversion rate

Ads charge per click, so every second of waiting is real money burning. Uncompressed images, too many tracking scripts, heavy animations — those are the three usual suspects. Target opening in **under 3 seconds on 4G**, not on office wifi.

**Takeaway:** Compress the images and remove every script whose report you don't read weekly.

## 8. Make several versions and test continuously

Nobody gets a landing page right first time. The practical rule is **one page per audience segment**: people searching on price, people comparing options, people who already know the brand — three different messages. The problem is that traditional approaches make this too expensive: every new page is another wait.

## 9. Measure first, optimise second

If you don't know how many people saw the page, clicked the button and submitted the form, every change is guesswork. Set up conversion tracking from day one, and more importantly: **reconcile the leads on the page against the leads your sales team actually called**. The gap between those two numbers usually exposes a technical fault nobody noticed.

**Takeaway:** Before running ads, fill in the form yourself once and check the data arrives where it should.

## Where to start

Don't try to apply all nine at once. Open your current landing page on a phone and check three things first: does the headline match the ad, how many fields does the form have, and how long does it take to load. Fixing those three is usually enough to show a visible difference in the first week.
