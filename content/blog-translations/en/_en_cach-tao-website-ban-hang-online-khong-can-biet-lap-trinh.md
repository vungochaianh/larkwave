A great many sellers stop at exactly one sentence: "I want my own website but I can't code." In practice, the hard part of an online shop isn't the code — it is whether you have thought clearly about what you sell, to whom, and where the orders go. Here is a seven-step route from idea to a real selling link, along with the places beginners tend to trip.

## 1. Decide the selling model before thinking about design

![Decide the selling model before thinking about design](https://apia.toolswave.net/uploads/images/1789672672450-ed6468ee.png)

Before opening any tool, answer four questions:

- **How many products** do you sell? Ten items and 500 items lead to two completely different websites.
- Do customers need a **cart** to buy several things at once, or just a "Buy now" button after which you call to confirm?
- Do products have **variants** (size, colour, volume)? This is the thing most often forgotten and most often forces a rebuild.
- What **payment** do you accept: bank transfer, cash on delivery, or an online payment gateway?

Write the four answers on paper. They are your blueprint for every step that follows.

## 2. Prepare the product data first

![Prepare the product data first](https://apia.toolswave.net/uploads/images/1789672744881-7cf119de.png)

Gather everything into one spreadsheet with these columns: product name, short description, price, category, stock, image filename.

A few small habits that save hours:

- Crop images to **one consistent ratio** (square 1:1 is safest), clean background, under 300KB each.
- Keep short descriptions to one or two sentences — don't paste the supplier's long blurb.
- Name image files after the product, no accents, no spaces.

Once this step is done, building the site is just mechanics.

## 3. Choose how to build it

### A drag-and-drop builder

The advantage is seeing results immediately. The drawback appears when you need your own logic: shipping fees by district, a cake order form with a delivery date, an admin page filtering orders by status. At that point you are boxed in by the available blocks.

### Hiring someone

Usually the best quality, but it costs money and time, and every time you want a word changed you wait.

### Describing the idea and letting AI build it

This is the newer route. On [Toolswave](/create-website), you type a description in plain language, for example *"build a coffee shop with a cart and an order admin page"*. The AI writes the source code, builds a real database and API, runs the build, shows you a preview and deploys it to a public link. There is no dragging of blocks and no prebuilt template to pick — the site is written fresh from your description.

## 4. Write a description with enough detail

The quality of the result depends almost entirely on the clarity of your description. Compare:

- **Vague:** "make a website to sell coffee"
- **Workable:** "build a coffee shop where each product has a photo, price, description and a size choice of S/M/L; include a cart; ordering collects name, phone and address; include an admin page listing orders and letting me change their status"

A useful trick: list things in three groups — **pages the customer sees**, **data that must be stored**, **admin pages**. Those three are the backbone of every online shop.

## 5. Preview it and place a test order yourself

Don't skip this. Play the customer and walk the whole sequence:

1. Add products to the cart, change quantities, remove one.
2. Place an order using your real phone number.
3. Open the admin page and check the order appears.
4. Do the whole thing again on a phone — most Vietnamese customers buy on mobile.

Don't expect the first version to be perfect. The effective way to revise is **one clear request at a time**: "add a notes field to orders", then "let me filter orders by status". A list of ten bullet points at once usually produces a messier result.

## 6. Deploy and attach your own domain

The default link works immediately for test sales and for sending to customers. Once you are serious, buy a domain and point it at the site. Toolswave deploys for real on Cloudflare infrastructure and supports custom domains, so you never rent hosting or touch server configuration. There is a free tier with no credit card, enough to try before you spend anything.

## 7. Connect payment and shipping

Plainly: this is the part no tool does entirely for you. Online payment gateways require a business licence and a company account, and couriers need their own contracts.

If you are starting out, the fastest route is showing a **bank account number with a transfer QR code**, or accepting **cash on delivery**. Orders arrive and you confirm them manually in the admin page. Only once volume is high does automatic integration make sense — that is when the integration cost starts to pay for itself.

## Conclusion

A website is not the destination, it is the machine that receives orders. Don't spend three months adjusting button colours. Launch something simple in an afternoon, let a few real customers use it, note where they hesitate, then expand exactly where they need it. An ugly website taking orders still beats a beautiful one that only exists in your head.
